title: How I work
---
I specialise in bringing ideas and designs to life using web technologies. Even during the concept phase, I provide input on data structures, user journeys and potential pitfalls. I give design teams early feedback on the effort involved and the implications of their decisions. Together, we refine the designs to achieve the project objectives. I also provide insight into how the code works and the underlying framework to foster mutual understanding.


## Web Standards

I have seen how the web has changed over time. But without standards and their ongoing development, this progress would not have been possible. As well as valid and semantic HTML, I place great importance on modern CSS. Supporting older browsers is nothing new to me, and I adapt my approach based on insights from analytics data or project requirements.

## Web Performance

Nobody likes waiting on websites, which is why performance has been a key focus for me right from the start of my career. Meeting the Core Web Vitals (Lighthouse) targets is just one aspect of many. I also look at other parts of the process and provide feedback on infrastructure, automation and the developer experience.

## Accessibility

All the knowledge in the world is now available at the click of a button. And it should be accessible to everyone. At some point, everyone faces temporary or permanent limitations, whether due to injury, age or their circumstances. That is why I champion accessibility in every project, including by sharing my knowledge with the team and our clients. When putting ideas into practice, avoiding barriers is always an essential part of my thinking and actions. When auditing the work of colleagues, I suggest specific changes and provide guidance on comprehensive refactoring.

## Iterative Process

If we first clarify what information and features are to be included, the bulk of the work can already be implemented in the code. This means the content is clear for the design. Particularly when time is short, I find it helpful to start by creating a basic version that includes everything that’s important. From there, we can iterate, for example by adding effects or interactivity.

## A mindful approach to language models

Language models, commonly referred to as AI, can be a great help during development. They remove the hurdle of having to know a CMS or framework inside out. However, this does not relieve us of the need to understand what we are doing. It is my own motivation not to relinquish control and to continue learning. This is particularly true in view of the social and environmental costs, as well as the moral dilemma, that the technology brings with it: loss of expertise, consumption of resources, and the origin of the training data. That is why I use it selectively and scrutinise the results critically.

## What I expect

I want to work with people who don’t just judge the end result by whether it looks nice. Qualities such as performance, accessibility, maintainability and the user experience should play key roles. If they motivate and inspire me to step out of my comfort zone, so much the better. Thanks to language models, I can make rapid progress even in unfamiliar tech stacks, but a team that shares its knowledge with one another is irreplaceable.
